## Hook library — proven patterns with examples

The hook is the first 1-3 seconds. It does one job: stop the scroll. Hook rate (3-sec views / impressions) tells you whether the hook works; nothing else matters if it doesn't. This reference is the pattern library every senior buyer (Barry Hott, Foxwell, CTC, Motion, Pilothouse) cycles through.

For each pattern: the structure, 5-10 examples drawn from real high-spend creatives, and when it works best in the funnel.

## Problem hook

Open by naming the problem the viewer is actively in. Works because Meta's algorithm targets people who have signaled relevance to the problem; the hook confirms the targeting.

**Structure:** `Stop / Are you / Tired of / Why are you still + <problem the viewer recognizes>`

**Examples:**

- "Stop wasting money on overpriced supplements that don't work."
- "Tired of meal-planning every Sunday? There's a better way."
- "Why are you still folding laundry by hand in 2026?"
- "If your CPA spiked this week, you're not alone."
- "Most agencies bill you for hours they didn't work. Here's how to know."
- "Your skincare routine has 12 products. You need 3."
- "Sleep less than 7 hours? This is for you."
- "Spending $200/mo on takeout? Read this."

**Works best:** top-of-funnel prospecting. Identifies the user with their own pain.

**Fails when:** the audience doesn't actually have the problem (over-broad targeting) or has fatigue on the problem framing (everyone in the niche says the same thing). Watch for hook rate spike on day 1 then fast decay — that's "everyone clicks, no one converts."

## Before/After hook

Show the transformation as the first frame. Compresses the value prop into one visual.

**Structure:** `I tried X for N days... / Here's what happened when I... / N days of X, here's the result`

**Examples:**

- "I tried this skincare line for 30 days. Watch."
- "30 days, 15 pounds. No diet."
- "I gave up coffee for 14 days. Here's what changed."
- "I switched my ad agency. ROAS doubled in 6 weeks."
- "I built my entire wardrobe from one brand. Here's what I learned."
- "30 days of using this — would I keep going?"
- "Before: $400/mo on groceries. After: $200. Same meals."

**Works best:** product categories with visible results — fitness, skincare, home, fashion. Less effective for SaaS / B2B unless the "before" is a screenshot of pain.

**Fails when:** the after is mundane or the before is staged. UGC outperforms studio here because the viewer trusts the unpolished version more.

## Claim-and-evidence hook

Lead with a specific, sharp claim, then immediately deliver evidence. The claim has to be specific enough to feel testable, not so specific that it sounds fake.

**Structure:** `<Specific claim with number> + <one visible piece of evidence>`

**Examples:**

- "Our customers see results in 14 days, not 60."
- "8 out of 10 buyers cancel their old agency within a month."
- "This replaced 6 products in my bathroom for $40."
- "47% lower CPA in 30 days. Here's how we did it."
- "The average customer saves $1,840/year on this."
- "9-second ads now outperform 30-second on Meta. Our data:"
- "We tested 47 hooks last quarter. Three patterns won."

**Works best:** middle-of-funnel — viewer is already aware of the category, wants proof.

**Fails when:** the claim is generic ("amazing results") or the evidence is missing. The pattern requires both halves.

## Social proof hook

Cite the volume, the cohort, or the recognizable buyer.

**Structure:** `Why N + <type of person> + <verb>...` or `<Recognizable name/brand> + <action>`

**Examples:**

- "Why 1,500 founders chose Brex over Mercury."
- "The skincare line dermatologists actually use."
- "10,000+ runners trusted this — here's why."
- "How Patagonia ships 90% of orders within 24 hours."
- "Why $500M+ DTC brands use this fulfillment platform."
- "The mattress 2,800 hotels switched to last year."
- "Most popular among CTOs at Series A startups."

**Works best:** top-of-funnel prospecting and trust-building in retargeting. Authority + volume converts skeptics.

**Fails when:** the number is unverifiable or the cohort is irrelevant ("Why 10,000 people loved this" — which 10,000?). Specificity carries.

## Founder POV hook

Founder on camera, named, vouching with personal credibility. Compounds with social proof patterns.

**Structure:** `Hi, I'm <name>. <Brief credential>. <Statement of belief or insight>.`

**Examples:**

- "I'm Andrew. After managing $60M+ in ad spend, I built this."
- "Hi, I'm Sarah. I started this company because I couldn't find a single..."
- "I've sold 10,000 of these. Here's what surprised me."
- "I'm not a marketer. I'm a chef who got tired of bad meal kits."
- "11 years running a beauty business, and this changed everything."
- "I'm Dave. I tested this on my own kids first."
- "Three years ago, my CPA was $80. Today it's $22. Here's what worked."

**Works best:** D2C with strong founder narrative. SaaS with technical-founder credibility. Anywhere "trust the brand" is the barrier.

**Fails when:** the founder reads as inauthentic or over-coached. The pattern requires the founder to be visibly themselves — uncomfortable, off-rhythm, even slightly awkward outperforms polished.

## Demonstration hook

Show the product doing the thing in the first frame. No talking yet — the action is the hook.

**Structure:** `<Visible action of product producing outcome>` — text overlay or voiceover joins by second 3.

**Examples:**

- (Visual) Knife sliding through tomato — text overlay: "We sharpened this once. Then waited 12 months."
- (Visual) Vacuum picking up coins, sand, pet hair in 4 seconds — voiceover: "Watch this."
- (Visual) Shower head transforming from regular to massage in 1 second — text: "One twist."
- (Visual) Two people unfolding a mattress out of a box in 30 seconds — text: "From box to bed."
- (Visual) Software dashboard auto-updating data in real time — text: "No more spreadsheets."
- (Visual) Phone screen showing one tap → full report — text: "From 4 hours to 4 seconds."

**Works best:** physical products with a wow factor. Fitness equipment, kitchen tools, home tech, software with visible UI improvement.

**Fails when:** the action takes longer than 2 seconds to deliver the payoff. The first frame must already be doing something.

## Pattern interrupt hook

Visual or audio surprise designed to break the scroll reflex.

**Structure:** Unusual visual, unexpected sound, abrupt cut, or breaking the fourth wall in the first 0.5 seconds.

**Examples:**

- Person on camera shouting "Wait — don't scroll past this."
- Sudden close-up of a face filling the frame, then pulls back to product.
- Glass shattering / pen click / unexpected sound effect under a soft visual.
- "Why are you watching this on mute? Turn the sound on."
- Footage of someone visibly throwing the product (then catching it).
- Bright color flash for 0.3 seconds before product reveal.
- Animated text typing fast: "DON'T BUY THIS YET."

**Works best:** when the audience has already seen the brand's standard creatives many times — fatigue setting in. Pattern interrupt resets attention.

**Fails when:** every ad in the rotation uses pattern interrupt — viewers learn to ignore the pattern itself. Use sparingly, rotate with quieter hooks.

## The "ugly ad" school (Barry Hott)

Hott's thesis (~$1B in spend behind it): native-looking, unpolished, low-production ads outperform studio polish on Meta prospecting. Hooks under this banner:

- Raw screen recording of a text-message exchange.
- Customer-style review screenshot held up to phone camera.
- Voice-memo style audio over a still image.
- A handwritten note photographed in mediocre lighting.
- Phone-screen-recording of an SMS / email / DM screenshot.
- Hand-drawn diagram on a whiteboard or napkin.

**Why it works:** the viewer's algorithm-tuned brain pattern-matches "ad" on production polish. Lower polish = lower ad-detection = scroll-stop.

**When to use:** prospecting cold. Less effective for retargeting where the viewer has already engaged with the brand.

## Patterns by funnel stage

| Stage | Best hook patterns |
|---|---|
| Cold prospecting | Problem; Pattern interrupt; Ugly ad; Founder POV |
| Warm prospecting | Before/After; Demonstration; Social proof |
| Retargeting (cart, browse) | Claim-and-evidence; Demonstration with offer overlay |
| Retargeting (existing customer) | Founder POV thanking customer; new-product Demonstration |
| Brand / awareness | Founder POV; Before/After (longer narrative); Social proof |

## Anti-patterns

- "Here at [Brand], we make..." — the corporate-website opener. Zero scroll-stop.
- Multi-second logo intro. By the time the logo finishes, the viewer scrolled.
- Slow-paced "story" hooks longer than 3 seconds. By 4 seconds, you've lost the scroll-skip cohort entirely.
- Hooks that don't connect to body. The transition from "stop the scroll" to "now deliver the value prop" is where 5-3-1 testing earns its keep.
- Reusing the same hook across 6 ads. Meta's algorithm rewards variance; reusing kills your refresh value.
